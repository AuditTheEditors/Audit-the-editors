How it works

What counts

When the host puts the same question to every editor at the table about something that gets resolved later (who wins Kansas, how many House seats the Democrats end up with, whether Hegseth is still at the Pentagon in January), each answer goes on the record with the audio.

No calls count as wrong

If an editor never picks a side on a pick question (“it's a coin flip”), declines to answer, or answers a different question (“if he survives two weeks, he survives through January”), it's a no call. A no call counts as wrong once the question is resolved. On a percent odds question, 50-50 is an answer: it's scored as 50%.

Types of questions and how each is scored

Picks
A winner, or yes or no. Correct or wrong. These are what the % counts.
Numbers
Like a seat count. Never simply “right”: graded by how far off it was (said 240 seats, the result was 236: off by 4) and ranked against the rest of the panel. Lower is better.
Percent odds
Analyzed with Brier score: the mean squared error across all percent odds questions. Lower is better, and 0 is perfect.

The scoreboard

The % is correct picks out of resolved picks, shown next to the counts behind it. No calls count as wrong. Numbers and odds don't move the %; they have their own columns (average seats off, average Brier score).

Resolving a question

Each question's resolution rule is written when the question is first asked, and it's shown with the question. When the result comes in, it's recorded with a link to its public source and checked by hand before any grade counts.
